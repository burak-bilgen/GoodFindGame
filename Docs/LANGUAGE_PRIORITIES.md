# Additional language priorities

Researched 2026-10-22. First wave: **German (`de`), then French (`fr`)**, alongside existing English/Turkish. This is a practical, evidence-informed localization decision, not a forecast that these will be the game's largest audiences. Keep one fictional market and identical simulation rules in every language.

No public source found establishes language-specific demand for this particular offline second-hand trading game. Country totals do not establish preferred language, acquisition cost, retention, or likely revenue. German/French prioritization is our inference from reachable mobile audiences, adjacent iOS games, and implementation cost. It is not an exhaustive global language ranking.

## Why this first wave is manageable

Both languages fit the current Latin-script, left-to-right interface. Two complete translations keep the ongoing catalog and editorial workload bounded. German compounds and French agreement/spacing still need attention; Latin script does not guarantee labels fit. Avoid launching a large batch of partial or literal translations.

Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese are candidates for the next research pass, using actual App Store impressions/downloads and early retention by territory once available. Japanese, Korean, Chinese and Arabic remain possible future languages, but need their own editorial/typographic review; Arabic additionally needs right-to-left layout work. This sequencing is an engineering scope choice, not evidence that these audiences are weaker.

## Translation policy

- Translate complete intent: natural seller/buyer bargaining, clear risk disclosures, repairs and profit/loss terminology. Use consistent informal German `du` and French `tu` unless an existing character voice warrants formality.
- Preserve each person's identity and index across locales. Keep recognizable proper names; translate descriptive fictional neighborhood/place names when this makes the world readable. Do not replace the cast with country-specific populations or imply a real city/economy.
- Keep brands, product identities, amounts, placeholders and game mechanics intact. Currency choice remains independent of language; a French/German translation does not require a new economy or a new exchange rate.
- Translate onboarding, notifications, accessibility copy, errors and achievements as well as marketplace dialogue. App Store and Game Center text require separate localized metadata work.
- Treat agent-authored translation as an editorial draft. Completion checks do not establish native fluency; native-player review remains desirable before public release.

## Focused acceptance, without repeated broad test runs

Run the catalog completeness/placeholder check once after the batch, then one relevant build and focused runtime/layout checks. Inspect onboarding, marketplace/bargaining, profit/loss, settings and competition in both languages, including largest text size and long strings. Check plural/zero counts and currency independently of language. Do not rerun unrelated suites unless a code change or failure justifies it.

Apple provides [double-length and bounded pseudolanguages](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/preparing-your-interface-for-localization) for expansion/truncation checks. [Apple's localization overview](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/localization) covers string catalogs and plural handling. [App Store metadata localization](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/localize-app-information) is separate from localizing the app binary; adding in-game strings alone does not localize the store listing.

## Follow-up: Arabic, Japanese, Chinese and Hindi

All four can broaden access. Population alone does not rank their value for an iOS trading simulation. Within this requested group, the recommended research/implementation sequence is **Japanese → Chinese (explicit script/territory choice) → Arabic → Hindi**. This is a judgment balancing evidence and delivery cost, not a measured conversion ranking. It does not require adding them all to the current German/French batch.

| Language | Evidence and implication | Additional work / decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Japanese (`ja`) | Sensor Tower reports over $10 billion of mobile-game IAP in the 12 months ending July 2026; iOS has the majority of spending and a slight lead in downloads. This is the strongest current, platform-specific opportunity signal among these four. Revenue leaders skew toward established titles, so the totals do not forecast our game's success. [Sensor Tower, Japan 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/state-of-japan-gaming-2026) | Best next candidate after the small current batch. Give bargaining dialogue a consistent natural register; inspect Japanese line wrapping, glyph sizes, cramped cards and truncation. Native editorial review matters. |
| Chinese — Simplified (`zh-Hans`) and Traditional (`zh-Hant`) | Sensor Tower's 2025 report identifies China, measured on iOS only, as the second-largest mobile IAP market in 2024. This supports serious consideration of Simplified Chinese, not a prediction for our niche. Apple's storefront table separately lists Simplified Chinese for China mainland and Traditional Chinese for Taiwan/Hong Kong/Macau. [Sensor Tower, March 2025](https://sensortower.com/press/press-release-sensor-tower-mobile-gaming-rebounds-in-2024-as-player-engagement-and-spending-reach-new-highs), [Apple storefront language table](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-store-localizations/) | Do not ship one generic `zh` translation. Select intended storefronts and review terminology independently for both scripts; automatic character conversion alone is insufficient editorial QA. Traditional Chinese can be scoped around Taiwan first. Review China mainland distribution separately before committing a mainland release; translation and distribution are separate tasks. No legal requirements are asserted here. |
| Arabic (`ar`) | Sensor Tower/Bidease reports GCC app IAP growing 41% from Q1 2024 to Q1 2026. This is an all-app, regional signal, not Arabic-language gaming demand or an iOS-only figure. It supports investigating the audience, without claiming all GCC users prefer Arabic. [2026 Middle East report](https://sensortower.com/blog/sensor-tower-bidease-2026-middle-east-app-growth-report) | Valuable candidate after a focused RTL pass. Apple says standard components may mirror automatically, but paragraphs, directional symbols and numerals still need appropriate treatment. Inspect mixed Latin brands/Arabic text, prices, negative profit, navigation and notifications. Use clear Modern Standard Arabic rather than pretending one dialect represents every country. [Apple RTL guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/right-to-left) |
| Hindi (`hi`) | India generated 8.45 billion mobile-game installs in FY2024–25 and simulation is a large-download genre. However Android dominates downloads, while iOS has higher-value spending. Those country/platform facts do not quantify the extra audience a Hindi translation adds over our existing English. [Sensor Tower India 2025 overview](https://sensortower.com/blog/india-mobile-game-insights-2025), [platform summary](https://sensortower.com/state-of-mobile-games-in-india-2025-report) | Keep on the backlog pending India iOS traction or user requests. No reliable primary-source split of Hindi versus English preference among Indian iOS gamers was found in this pass: do not claim most use either language. India is multilingual; Hindi is not equivalent to “Indian localization.” Check Devanagari shaping, font sizes and line breaks when implemented. |

Apple supports metadata in all these languages, including Hindi and both Chinese scripts; a storefront's default language is not evidence of what every user prefers. Device settings and available app localizations also affect display. [Apple App Store localizations](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-store-localizations/)

For every candidate, preserve the shared fictional world and economy. Localized names, typography and currency presentation do not create country-specific gameplay. Revisit the sequence when storefront impressions, downloads, language requests and retention provide game-specific evidence.
