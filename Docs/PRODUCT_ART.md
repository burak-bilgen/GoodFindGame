# Generated marketplace photography

22 October 2026. The owner explicitly requested generated realistic product
imagery and replacement of the app icon. This visual follow-up does not authorize
broader progression or monetization work. The original clean checkpoint and
living-marketplace behavior checkpoints remain recorded in their own reports.

## Art direction and generation

Generated with the built-in imagegen tool. Fictional unbranded used electronics,
photographic materials, soft natural upper-left window light, a warm neutral desk,
consistent three-quarter framing and generous margins. Four photographs of the
same product form a 2x2 atlas: excellent, good, fair, poor in reading order. Only
cosmetic scuffs, surface scratches, rubbed finish and discoloration change.
Controls stay intact. No real logos, text, internal faults or hidden defects are
baked into the photographs. Exact generation prompts and source references are
recorded with the final art manifest.

The icon prompt requests one bold amber 3D price tag with a thick ivory flipping
arrow on midnight petrol blue, no text/coins/currency symbols/border, full-bleed
opaque square with no rounded outer mask. The selected result is packaged at
1024 square, with no alpha. Original generation remains outside the checkout;
the app consumes the committed asset catalog copy.

Scripts/package_product_atlas.swift only slices the generated four-photo atlas
into ordinary compressed JPEG image sets. It does not repaint, retouch or invent
wear. Each photo is loaded independently so the app need not decode a whole atlas.
Final photos are 623 square pixels; 56 JPEGs total 5,981,052 bytes. The two-pixel
trim around each quadrant removes any generated separator line.

## Selection and limits

The current catalog uses family-level photography, not a fabricated claim of
50 independently photographed model designs. ProductPhoto maps every seeded
model to an appropriate form; gaming-15 is a screen-equipped handheld. The same
form remains stable across advertised and owned views. Existing model names,
prices, saved truth and simulation behavior do not change.

Wide image frames extend the same photograph’s blurred lighting at the sides;
the sharp foreground preserves the complete product without stretching or cropping
controls.

The renderer selects a cosmetic grade from ItemAppearance. Advertised pictures
use seller claims; uninspected inventory retains the seller image. Earned
inspection can change the cosmetic photograph to observed wear. The neutral
fallback uses good cosmetic condition without reading truth. Battery, boards,
ports and lens findings never select a photograph. Specific discovered faults
remain in the inspection report; photos do not pretend to be diagnostic closeups.
The former illustrative renderer remains a fallback for future unknown types.

Generation packaging keeps separate images rather than decoding four-photo
atlases at runtime. Image content is decorative for accessibility; existing item
names, condition evidence and inspection text remain accessible.

Cosmetic sets are shared by product family; individual models within a family do
not yet have unique body designs. This is a known limit, not evidence that the
entire visual or enjoyment goal is finished. Human playtesting must still establish
whether scanning these listings is interesting and the repeated loop is enjoyable.

The obsolete procedural icon-rendering script was removed to prevent accidentally
overwriting the generated replacement. Git retains the earlier icon and tool.
