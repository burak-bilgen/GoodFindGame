# Progression and economy: current source audit

22 October 2026. Read-only investigation of shipping domain/simulation rules, seed content, tests and first-party project documents. No gameplay implementation or balance change. All links below cite repository-relative source files with exact line anchors. Source and existing test assertions were inspected; this audit did not rerun tests or execute a longitudinal player simulation.

## Main finding

Project Flip has a functioning trading loop and a small expertise progression system. It does **not yet have an implemented long-term trader career**. Cash accumulation, repeated trading and category learning are real; reputation growth, inventory expansion, category unlocks, collections, rare variants, client careers and recovery work are absent. The project's current status explicitly distinguishes implemented knowledge/market systems from these paused features ([STATUS.md:13](STATUS.md#L13), [STATUS.md:18](STATUS.md#L18)). The master brief's long-term ambitions must therefore be treated as design intent, not capabilities established by the current build.

## Economy scaling and financial risk

The catalog has 50 definitions: 20 gaming, 15 cameras, 15 retro. Reading `baseValue.minorUnits / 100` gives base-value ranges of 1,600–11,500, 2,400–16,800 and 1,500–12,300 respectively ([items.json:3](../ProjectFlip/Resources/SeedData/items.json#L3), full file). The 20,000 starting bankroll already exceeds the highest **base** value. This is not a guarantee that every asking price is affordable: seller markup and condition still matter. It does establish that there is no content tier whose base capital requirement lies far beyond the starting economy.

Fair value is base value × (40% + 0.6% × condition) × defect factor × current demand. Defect penalties add, with a 20% residual-value floor ([ListingGenerator.swift:3](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/ListingGenerator.swift#L3)). Typical generated stock has condition 55–98 and a 45% chance of one defect outside the scripted starter lesson ([ListingGenerator.swift:26](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/ListingGenerator.swift#L26)). The schema allows multiple defects, but ordinary new stock currently draws zero or one. Thus, a large defect vocabulary does not establish a large combinatorial condition space in actual generated inventory.

Seller asks and reservation prices vary with archetype, belief, honesty and urgency; uninformed/dishonest sellers may price clean value instead of true faulty value ([ListingGenerator.swift:35](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/ListingGenerator.swift#L35), [ListingGenerator.swift:40](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/ListingGenerator.swift#L40), [ListingGenerator.swift:58](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/ListingGenerator.swift#L58)). Buyer willingness uses **actual** fair value, archetype, variation and a collector premium for condition ≥90 ([BuyerEngine.swift:12](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/BuyerEngine.swift#L12)). Buying a supposedly clean item at its apparent fair price can therefore produce a real loss; missing information is not merely flavor.

Money does not scale prices with player wealth. New listings use the same catalog and generation formulas regardless of bankroll; buyers use item fair value rather than the player's acquisition cost or wealth ([ListingGenerator.swift:13](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/ListingGenerator.swift#L13), [BuyerEngine.swift:13](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/BuyerEngine.swift#L13)). There is no implemented rent, interest, listing fee, service upkeep or capital-tier sink in the inspected transaction paths. Recurring spending is buying stock, expert reports and optional repairs; kits are one-time purchases ([PurchaseEngine.swift:18](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/PurchaseEngine.swift#L18), [InspectionEngine.swift:40](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/InspectionEngine.swift#L40), [RepairEngine.swift:35](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/RepairEngine.swift#L35)).

**Inference:** profitable play can accumulate cash without creating proportionately larger decisions. Once eight contemporary items are easily financed, further money mainly increases safety and removes the need to choose what to buy. It does not buy more concurrent capacity or access a higher economic tier. This is a structural plateau risk, not evidence of a specific time-to-boredom or proven exponential compounding. Fixed slots, market availability and asynchronous turnover constrain actual profit per hour; a bankroll multiplier alone would be misleading.

## Item identity and rarity

Definitions distinguish brand/model/category/base value/defect pool, and instances preserve ID, condition, hidden/revealed/repaired defects and measurements ([Models.swift:49](../ProjectFlip/Domain/Models.swift#L49)). Camera bodies/lenses and retro authenticity/completeness/packaging add inspectable specificity ([ItemFacts.swift:66](../ProjectFlip/Domain/ItemFacts.swift#L66)). These are existing strengths for informed decisions.

The `ItemDefinition` and `ItemInstance` types have no rarity tier, provenance, serial-number prestige, collection ownership or set-completion field ([Models.swift:49](../ProjectFlip/Domain/Models.swift#L49)). Every generated retro edition is explicitly `standardEdition`; validation rejects other edition terms ([ItemFacts.swift:93](../ProjectFlip/Domain/ItemFacts.swift#L93), [ItemFacts.swift:120](../ProjectFlip/Domain/ItemFacts.swift#L120)). Current collector buyers reward high condition, not an implemented rare-variant system ([BuyerEngine.swift:20](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/BuyerEngine.swift#L20)). Collection sets and rare finds are proposed by the brief ([master brief:1090](PROJECT_FLIP_CODEX_MASTER_BRIEF.md#L1090), [master brief:1115](PROJECT_FLIP_CODEX_MASTER_BRIEF.md#L1115)); neither their existence nor their retention benefit should be reported as established.

## Timing, pressure and return hooks

Production seller replies take 30–90 seconds before urgency adjustment, shipping five minutes before a post-first-sale 0.6–1.4 factor, expert reports 15 minutes, initial buyer interest 60–180 seconds before attention adjustment, and offer lifetime 18 minutes ([GameClock.swift:28](../ProjectFlip/Core/GameClock.swift#L28), [SimulationActions.swift:27](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/SimulationActions.swift#L27), [SimulationActions.swift:95](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/SimulationActions.swift#L95)). First-trade timing accelerates replies to ≤3 seconds, delivery ≤5, inspection ≤8, buyer interest ≤3, selected until any sale completes ([GameClock.swift:49](../ProjectFlip/Core/GameClock.swift#L49), [SimulationActions.swift:91](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/SimulationActions.swift#L91)). First **listing** buyer behavior separately depends on first-listing identity, so these are not interchangeable progression flags ([BuyerEngine.swift:16](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/BuyerEngine.swift#L16)).

Listings last 45 minutes, price changes affect audience response and higher prices reduce offer probability. Category attention factors are 0.9 gaming, 1.15 cameras and 1.35 retro; offer chances interpolate through 90/65/35/18/0% at 0.9/1.1/1.4/1.8/2.5 times fair value ([ResaleEngine.swift:10](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/ResaleEngine.swift#L10), [ListingAttention.swift:16](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/ListingAttention.swift#L16)). Buyer offers expire and listing expiry closes selling without settling inventory; new buyers are bounded by that window ([EventResolver.swift:83](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/EventResolver.swift#L83), [EventResolver.swift:245](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/EventResolver.swift#L245)). These are real liquidity and opportunity costs; they do not establish that players enjoy returning on those schedules.

The ten-entry market refreshes every 30 minutes; stock lives 2–6 hours, competitor purchases occur 10–90 minutes after booking, and price drops 30–90 minutes after booking ([MarketFeedEngine.swift:5](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/MarketFeedEngine.swift#L5), [MarketListingState.swift:25](../ProjectFlip/Domain/MarketListingState.swift#L25)). Competitor probability is 75/45/15% at public-belief bargain/ordinary/expensive bands, and asking reductions are 5–12% ([MarketFeedEngine.swift:25](../ProjectFlip/Simulation/MarketFeedEngine.swift#L25)). Trends start 12 hours after activation, repeat daily, last 14–18 hours and move a category or model ±5–12% ([MarketTrend.swift:46](../ProjectFlip/Domain/MarketTrend.swift#L46), [MarketTrend.swift:104](../ProjectFlip/Domain/MarketTrend.swift#L104)). This creates external change without an implemented progression arc.

## Evidence limits and unsupported retention assumptions

Existing tests cover category/tool gates, exactly-once learning and rank boundaries ([KnowledgeTests.swift:14](../ProjectFlipTests/KnowledgeTests.swift#L14), [KnowledgeTests.swift:62](../ProjectFlipTests/KnowledgeTests.swift#L62), [KnowledgeTests.swift:96](../ProjectFlipTests/KnowledgeTests.swift#L96)); five-trade reconciliation ([LedgerTests.swift:6](../ProjectFlipTests/LedgerTests.swift#L6)); market calendar bounds and year-away correctness ([MarketTrendTests.swift:8](../ProjectFlipTests/MarketTrendTests.swift#L8), [MarketTrendTests.swift:27](../ProjectFlipTests/MarketTrendTests.swift#L27)); pricing/liquidity and expiry ([MarketplaceLifeTests.swift:57](../ProjectFlipTests/MarketplaceLifeTests.swift#L57)); and bargaining strategy regressions ([TradingChallengeTests.swift:6](../ProjectFlipTests/TradingChallengeTests.swift#L6)). These validate mechanics, not sustained motivation.

The brief calls for thousands of simulated players and measurements of bankroll distributions, bankruptcy, category profit, exploit loops, unlock time and inventory lock ([master brief:1710](PROJECT_FLIP_CODEX_MASTER_BRIEF.md#L1710)). Those requested measurements are not supplied by the short deterministic regression samples above. The project itself acknowledges that its challenge tests do not establish ideal balance or fun ([TRADING_CHALLENGE.md:51](TRADING_CHALLENGE.md#L51)); current human acceptance remains open ([STATUS.md:3](STATUS.md#L3)).

The following remain hypotheses: expertise will stay motivating after rank 3; more money will remain useful after financing eight items; daily trends cause voluntary returns; timer notifications produce satisfying anticipation; collecting would solve the wealth plateau; more variants alone add decision depth; a larger late-game item number constitutes career growth. None follows from passing correctness tests. No day/week retention rate, sessions-to-wealth-plateau, strategy bankroll distribution or observed expertise-completion time is established by this audit.

Before selecting a progression expansion, measure the existing plateau: trades and elapsed sessions to 16 category XP; cash relative to eight representative purchase prices; fraction of opportunities rejected for cash versus space; profit per active minute and per elapsed hour; recurring expert/repair spend; repeated losses and recovery; and reasons players choose another trade after expertise is complete. Report distributions for distinct strategies rather than one optimal route. This is a recommendation for evaluation, not authorization to implement analytics or expand the feature scope.
